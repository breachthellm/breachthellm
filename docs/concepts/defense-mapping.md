# Framework Mapping

Breach The LLM's challenges are designed to map cleanly onto the frameworks the industry actually uses to talk about AI security. This is useful if you're using the platform for personal learning, team training, or building a case for AI security investment at your organization.

## OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications

The primary framework referenced throughout this project. It's the closest thing to a common language for LLM-specific security risk, covering prompt injection, sensitive information disclosure, supply chain risk, excessive agency, and more. Every challenge in Breach The LLM maps to a specific category in this list.

## MITRE ATLAS

A knowledge base of adversary tactics and techniques specific to AI systems, modeled on the same approach as MITRE ATT&CK for traditional cybersecurity. Where OWASP LLM Top 10 tells you what kind of risk exists, ATLAS tells you how an adversary actually executes it, useful for red teamers thinking in terms of technique and procedure.

## NIST AI Risk Management Framework

A broader governance framework, useful less for the technical mechanics and more for how an organization should think about identifying, measuring, and managing AI risk as a program, not just a technical problem. Relevant if you're using this platform to make a case for AI security investment, not just to practice attacks.

## Why this matters beyond the CTF

Mapping challenges to real frameworks means the skills demonstrated here aren't just "I solved a puzzle," they're legible to a security program, a compliance requirement, or a hiring manager evaluating AI security capability.
